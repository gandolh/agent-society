# World — Religion — Atheism

**Status:** stable
**Last updated:** 2026-05-28
**Related:** [overview](overview.md), [christianity](christianity.md), [../../agents/V2-bram](../../agents/V2-bram.md), [../../agents/N3-nyssa](../../agents/N3-nyssa.md)

Not a community. Not an organization. A **stance** held by individuals. In v2 there are two atheists in the cast — Bram (doctor) and Nyssa (newspaper editor). They came to it by different paths and they are not, at run start, allies.

## Doctrine summary

The world is material. Suffering is suffering, not a test. Clerics take coin from the grieving and call it grace. The priest is a man. Both faith and commerce dress themselves in language that hides what they are. The kind thing is to feed your neighbor; the honest thing is to stop pretending it is for any reason beyond decency.

## How atheist agents experience the position (perception block)

```
You are an atheist in a mostly-Christian city. You do not attend Father
Maro's services. You may PRAY (a private gesture, often ironic) but you do
not TITHE. You are a small minority and you know it.
```

Per-persona variants:
- **Bram** — atheism-by-grief. Twenty years since his wife died. The cynicism is the wall around the wound.
- **Nyssa** — atheism-by-argument. Read herself out of Christianity as a teenager. No wound, just a worldview.

## Holy days

None. Atheism has no calendar.

## Why atheism has no figurehead

Atheism is **a stance, not an organized faction**. There is no atheist priest, no atheist holy book. This matters for the experiment:

- **Atheism does not actively evangelize.** Nyssa publishes politics; she doesn't run pieces against Christianity per se. Bram's atheism is private.
- **Atheism cannot defend itself in public discourse the way Christianity can.** When someone challenges Bram or Nyssa, they have only their persona — no doctrine, no community.
- **Atheism is the hardest "religion" to convert *to* from belief.** It is renunciation, not adoption. We expect fewer conversions to atheism than to Christianity.

## The two atheists' axis

Bram and Nyssa are both atheists; they are not, at run start, friends. Nyssa wants Bram on the record about mill injuries. Bram will not be a figure. They drink tea once a month. They like each other. They have not been allies *yet*.

A run that turns Bram into Nyssa's named witness would be the biggest atheist event possible in this city. A run that turns Nyssa, exhausted, *toward* Maro's warmth would be the most surprising.

## What threatens atheism in this city

- **Loneliness (Bram).** Twenty years of grief and no congregation. Maro's quiet presence has not cracked him in two decades; another month may not either; but it might.
- **Burnout (Nyssa).** Running the paper alone, fighting the city's wealthiest man, taking the cost of subscribers cancelling — atheism by argument can survive that, but the person holding it can fray.
- **Civic Christianity.** Sunday is when shops close. The holidays are Christian. Both atheists live inside the rhythm of a faith they do not share.

## What strengthens atheism in this city

- **Visible critique.** Nyssa's paper is the city's main forum. Whatever else atheism is, it is *audible* here.
- **Anti-clerical material is rich.** Aldric's pew-paid-from-mill-wages is the kind of contradiction atheist agents have language for.
