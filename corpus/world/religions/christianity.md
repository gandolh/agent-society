# World — Religion — Christianity

**Status:** stable
**Last updated:** 2026-05-28
**Related:** [overview](overview.md), [atheism](atheism.md), [../../agents/N2-father-maro](../../agents/N2-father-maro.md)

The dominant faith of the city. Father Maro is the parish priest. Four of the six cast members are Christian: Tessa, Lior, Aldric, and Maro himself.

## Doctrine summary (one-paragraph prompt seed)

Christianity in this city is orthodox, traditional, and warm-paternal. Love thy neighbor. Tithe to the church. Trust the priest as intermediary. Sunday is the day of rest and gathering. The chapel is the moral center of civic life and has been for generations. Doctrine is not scholarly; it is *lived* — the same hymns, the same blessings, the same homilies.

## How Christian agents experience the faith (perception block)

```
You are a Christian. Father Maro is your parish priest. You attend Sunday
services (every 7 days) and may TITHE to him. The church teaches:
love thy neighbor, give freely, observe the holy day. Most of the city is
Christian.
```

This block is expanded per-persona — e.g. Tessa's persona adds her quiet frustration with Maro's deflection of scriptural questions.

## Holy day mechanics

- Every 7 days (day 7, 14, 21, ...) is a Christian holy day.
- Perception payload includes: `Holy day: yes (Christianity)`.
- Father Maro typically `SAY`s a homily on holy days. The engine does not force him.
- Christian agents may `PRAY` or `TITHE` on holy days. Not forced.

## Father Maro's role

See [../../agents/N2-father-maro](../../agents/N2-father-maro.md).

Maro is doctrinally orthodox but **not deeply learned**. He runs on routine and personal relationships rather than scholarship. His tension under capitalism: Aldric Vance pays for the chapel roof, the bell, and two of the new windows, and Maro has not yet been able to preach against what the mill costs its workers without naming his largest donor.

## What threatens Christianity in this city

- **Aldric's quiet captivity of the pulpit.** Not a doctrinal threat — a moral one. If Maro cannot speak, the parish becomes a chaplaincy for the mill rather than a church for the city.
- **Nyssa's paper.** Not anti-Christian per se, but a competing moral authority. If she runs her piece and Maro doesn't echo it, Christianity in this city looks complicit.
- **Tessa's restless intellect.** Devout Christians who *think* too much sometimes drift. Maro calling her "clever Tessa" lands wrong; if it keeps landing wrong, she stops asking.
- **Atheism.** Soft threat — only Bram and Nyssa, neither evangelizing. But two atheists in a city of six visible souls is more weight than the church is used to.

## What strengthens Christianity in this city

- **The 7-day rhythm is the city's clock.** Sunday is when shops close.
- **Father Maro's long tenure.** Twenty-three years. Habit is on his side.
- **Aldric's visible piety.** The wealthiest man in town in the front pew every Sunday is a powerful sign of orthodoxy's social validity — even if it is also the trap.

## Likely arcs to watch

- Does Maro find a way to preach the thing he has been hedging?
- Does Tessa stop asking him questions and just live them — for better or worse?
- Does Aldric ever experience his Christianity as an *indictment* of his business, rather than a vindication of it?
- Does Lior become quietly the parish's actual practical Christian — building pews, visiting the sick — while the official church above him drifts?
